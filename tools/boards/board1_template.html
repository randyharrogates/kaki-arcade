Finish, lighting and camera for Tabao Rush

You already chose chunky toy cooks, scripted, with one body and eight heads. This board settles the three things left: how shiny, what time of day, and how steeply the camera looks down. Each option is the same kitchen and the same eight cooks, rendered at true game size, 1920 × 1080.

Reply withA, B, C or D, or a mix: for example “B's finish and light at A's tilt”. Then I render one confirmation frame before any art is produced. All four cost the same at run time: they end up as flat sprites and one background image.

The four options

A

Glossy · day · 50°

  • Bright and clean; colours read strongly from the sofa.
  • The tilt used in everything you have seen so far.
  • Shiny highlights can look plasticky, especially on heads.
B

Matte · evening · 50°

  • Warm lamp light gives the most atmosphere; the burning wok glows.
  • Matte reads as hand-made, closer to the nostalgic mood.
  • Lower contrast than daylight: station states lean more on the HUD labels.
  • Player colours are slightly less pure under warm light.
C

Matte · day · 60°

  • Steeper camera: you see more of what sits on counters and less is hidden behind them.
  • Matte with daylight keeps colours true and removes the plastic shine.
  • Cooks show more hair and less face; they look a little shorter.
Recommended for play
D

Glossy · day · 42°

  • Lower camera: the most face and body, the most character.
  • Counters hide more of the floor behind them, and a cook behind a counter is half covered.
  • The kitchen needs more depth to fit, so everything is drawn slightly smaller in later levels.

What the three settings trade

SettingOne wayThe other way
FinishGlossy: punchy, toy-shop lookMatte: softer, hand-made look, no glare spots
LightDay: clearest, truest coloursEvening: most mood, lower contrast
TiltSteeper (60°): clearer play, less hiddenLower (42°): more character, more hidden

A level could also change the light by venue: day for the kopitiam, evening for the pasar malam. Say so if you want that; it costs one extra background render per level and nothing at run time.
